Case study · Order intake automation · 2026

From a shared orders inbox to the ERP, with people only on the exceptions

A UK food and drink brand received wholesale and retail orders by email in dozens of formats: PDFs, spreadsheets, EDI files, portal notices and plain-text replies. A small desk keyed each one into the ERP by hand.

I designed, built and run the system that now sorts, reads and checks every email in that inbox.

Illustrative run · no client data
  1. 01

    Watch

    Subscribes to the Outlook inbox through Microsoft Graph. Every email becomes a tracked job, so nothing is lost or handled twice.

  2. 02

    Sort

    Separates new orders from customer replies, portal notices, invoices and newsletters. Replies go to a person, never auto-closed.

  3. 03

    Read

    Fixed readers for each customer's layout; Claude reads the formats that don't fit a template.

  4. 04

    Check

    A gate compares the order with the original document: customer, products, quantities, dates and duplicates.

  5. 05

    Act

    Creates the sales order in the ERP, or holds it on a dashboard with the reason in plain English.

Clean orders

Created in the ERP automatically. Auto-send is switched on one customer at a time, once their format has passed a replay on real past orders.

Exceptions

Held on a "Waiting on you" dashboard with the document, what was read and why it stopped. The desk approves; the system never clears its own holds.

What made it work

Criteria before code

For every change I wrote what "correct" means first, then replayed it on the real email history before switching it on.

The desk's past work as the answer key

Each reader was checked against how people had actually keyed that customer's orders, so mistakes showed up as numbers, not opinions.

Safety over speed

Duplicate protection, a dispatch gate and a daily audit of the gate's decisions came before broader automation.

Monitoring built in

Failures are logged and posted to Slack; a daily check reports holds, replies and anything drifting.
